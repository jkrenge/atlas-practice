Sources and credits

Every map starts with somebody else's survey. Here are ours.

United States and Germany

Boundaries: Natural Earth, Admin 1 states and provinces, version 5.1.1, at 1:10 million. State maps exclude mapped lakes using Natural Earth Lakes, version 5.0.0. Boundaries are simplified for learning.

The World map, the map behind the app, and the map behind these pages are drawn from Natural Earth Admin 0 countries, version 5.1.1, at 1:50 million. The World map asks for the 193 UN member states, the two UN observer states, Kosovo and Taiwan.

Beat the clock on the World map uses 148 major cities from Natural Earth populated places, version 5.1.2.

License: public domain. Made with Natural Earth.

New York City boroughs

Borough boundaries: NYC Department of City Planning (DCP), Borough Boundaries on NYC Open Data, shore-clipped, version 26B. These are the five official boroughs.

Terms: NYC Open Data terms of use. The City provides the data without warranties of accuracy or fitness.

New York City neighborhoods

Contains information from NYC Neighborhoods by Pediacities / Ontodia, made available under the Open Data Commons Attribution License (ODC-By). The geometry is normalized from a pinned public GeoJSON mirror.

What we changed: two self-intersecting outlines were repaired; the neighborhoods outside Manhattan were combined into practice areas (West Brooklyn, South & East Brooklyn, and Queens (other areas)), with ten areas kept separate; every area was clipped to the official DCP borough shorelines, version 26B; and Seaport was drawn by hand out of the Financial District, along Pearl Street, John Street, the Brooklyn Bridge and the East River, approximately. The Bronx and Staten Island use the official borough boundaries.

A known limit: the datHere catalog credits Ontodia and declares the ODC-By license, but its download could not be compared with the mirror, so exact source equivalence remains unconfirmed. Neighborhood names and inland divisions are approximate and include parks and islands.

Facts, cities and sights

State capitals, US state nicknames, and the coordinates of capitals, cities and sights: Wikidata, CC0.

German state names and slogans: one sourced phrase per state, labeled by kind (nickname, state brand, tourism slogan or campaign). Each one links to its own source in the app.

Sights are a curated collection of 50 US and 20 German landmarks, plus 56 New York City sights for Beat the clock, picked for learning and geographic variety, not a popularity ranking. Streets and large parks use the source's reference point.

City populations

United States: U.S. Census Bureau, Vintage 2025 population estimates, July 1, 2025. The 50 largest cities.

Germany: Statistisches Bundesamt (Destatis), city populations, December 31, 2024, under the Datenlizenz Deutschland – Namensnennung – Version 2.0. The 10 largest cities, selected by Atlas.

The figures use city or municipal boundaries, not metro areas. Estimates may be revised in later releases.

Flags

All 66 state flags, 50 American and 16 German, are images from Wikimedia Commons. 65 are in the public domain. The flag of Mississippi is marked “copyrighted free use” and is credited to Rocky Vaughn, Sue Anna Joe, Dominique Pugh, Clay Moss, Kara Giles, Micah Whitson and the Mississippi Department of Archives and History (file page).

The app shows each flag's author, license and Commons file page in Browse facts.

Map layout

Alaska and Hawaii appear in separate insets at different scales. The maps use geographic coordinates. They are meant for learning, not navigation.
