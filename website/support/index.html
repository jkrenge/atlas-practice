Help and support

Short answers to real questions. Still stuck? Open an issue.

Rounds and settings

How do I change the number of questions or the timer?

Open Settings at the bottom of the home screen. Under Practice rounds, pick 10 or 25 questions, or All, and set the timer to Off, 30 s or 60 s per question. The settings apply to every map.

Out of the box, rounds have 10 questions and 30 seconds each.

Can I pause a timed question?

Yes. With the timer on, the pause button sits next to the countdown. The clock also stops on its own when you leave the app and while an answer is on screen.

Why did a question I missed come back?

That's the rematch. A missed question returns once, a couple of questions later, so it has a chance to stick. Your score counts first tries and rematches separately.

My pin was close. Why didn't it count?

A pin counts when it lands within 50 km of the answer on the US map, or within 20 km on the German map. Distance is measured on the globe, not on the screen, so zooming in doesn't change it. If you were just outside the circle, the app says so.

Your progress

Where is my progress stored?

Only on your device, in the app's own storage. Atlas keeps a score for each map and way to play, and doesn't send it anywhere. Like other app data, it is part of your device's own backups.

Progress doesn't sync between your iPhone and iPad; each device keeps its own.

How do I reset my progress?

Open Settings and tap Reset progress under Your progress. This deletes the scores for every map and mode on this device and can't be undone. Your round length and timer stay as they are.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-up and no login. Open the app and pick a map.

Maps and boundaries

Why don't the boundaries match other maps exactly?

Atlas uses learning maps, not survey maps. US and German state boundaries come from Natural Earth at a scale of 1:10 million and are simplified further so they draw quickly. Mapped lakes are cut out. Alaska and Hawaii sit in their own insets, at different scales from the rest of the US.

They're good for learning where things are. Please don't navigate with them.

Why are some New York City neighborhoods grouped together?

New York has no official neighborhood boundaries. Atlas uses historical neighborhood outlines from Pediacities/Ontodia, trimmed to the official borough shorelines from NYC City Planning.

Manhattan keeps its neighborhoods. Elsewhere, ten well-known areas stay separate, such as Williamsburg, DUMBO and Long Island City, and the rest are combined into practice areas: West Brooklyn, South & East Brooklyn, and Queens (other areas). The Bronx and Staten Island each play as one whole borough.

Who drew Seaport?

We did, by hand. None of the sources has a Seaport boundary, so it is cut out of the Financial District along Pearl Street and John Street, with the Brooklyn Bridge and the East River on the other sides. Expect it to be right to within roughly a block.

I found a wrong fact or a boundary that looks off.

Thank you. Every fact in Browse facts links to its source, which helps us check. Open a correction with the map, the question, and what you expected to see.

Devices and access

Does Atlas work offline?

Yes. Maps, facts and flags are all inside the app. The links to sources, this page and the privacy policy open in your browser and need a connection. So does sending feedback. When you are online, the app also sends crash reports and usage figures; the privacy policy says what they contain.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes. Atlas runs on iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 18 or later, in portrait and landscape.

Can I play without tapping the map?

In Find the area, Use area list lets you pick the answer from a searchable list. In pin questions, Coordinates lets you type a latitude and longitude instead of dragging a pin.

Atlas supports VoiceOver, larger text sizes and Reduce Motion. The list is a way to answer, not a full replacement for seeing the map; we're happy to hear how it works for you.

Get in touch

Questions, a capital we got wrong, a neighborhood you'd draw differently: open an issue on GitHub. It takes a free GitHub account. Issues are public, so leave out anything personal.

Report a problem or a correction

It helps to include your device, its iOS version, and the app version from Settings, under About. You can also browse existing issues.
