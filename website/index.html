A pin counts within 50 km in the US and 20 km in Germany. The cities are the 50 largest in the US and the 10 largest in Germany; the sights are 50 American and 20 German landmarks. Beat the clock is on every map: 15 places, 15 seconds each, and a high score to beat. The world asks for 148 major cities, New York City for 56 sights.

New York City

5 boroughs · 53 areas

Two maps of the city. Start with the five boroughs, then zoom in on the neighborhoods: 38 areas in Manhattan and 15 across the other boroughs.

Both maps play Find the area and Name the area. Some neighborhoods travel in groups, like West Brooklyn and Queens (other areas), and Seaport is drawn by hand. Boundaries are approximate, as any New Yorker will happily tell you.

How a round works

  1. Pick a map and a way to play. A round is 10 or 25 questions, or all of them. You choose in Settings.

  2. Beat the clock, or skip it. Take 30 or 60 seconds per question, or turn the timer off. Pause whenever you like; the clock waits.

  3. Miss one, meet it again. A missed question comes back once for a rematch, a couple of questions later.

  4. See how you did. Your first-try score earns a rank: Cartographer, Navigator, Explorer or Wanderer. Anything you missed is listed under Worth another look.

Read up first

Browse facts · Explore map

Every capital, flag, nickname, city and sight is there to read before you play, each with a link to its source. Flags come with their file credits.

Explore map lets you tap an area for its name and pinch to get closer. No quiz, promise.

Offline and private

No account needed

Maps, facts and flags all live inside the app, so Atlas works anywhere: on a plane, underground, at the edge of the map.

There is no account and no tracking. Your scores and settings stay on your device, and Reset progress in Settings wipes the slate clean.
